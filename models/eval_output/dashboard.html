# Distributed Spawns Close the Split-vs-Direct Gap (2026-08-30, DD-17)

**Hypothesis (from the prior tick):** the residual K=3 split-vs-direct gap is
STRUCTURAL — defenders spawn clustered at the target centroid and can't
pre-position across 3 spread points against an all-at-once rush. Fix: distribute
defender spawns round-robin across the K targets (env change, commit faa82d3).

## CONFIRMED — the gap is essentially closed
The full arc on K=3 direct simultaneous raids:

| Model / env | 10v10 | 15v15 | 20v20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| aegis_split (clustered, no direct training) | 81% | 78% | 74% |
| aegis_splitdirect (clustered, direct-trained) | 83% | 85% | 83% |
| **any split model + DISTRIBUTED spawns** | **90–93%** | **95%** | **95%** |

Saturation raids stay 100%; single-target stays 100% (no regression).
Time-to-first-intercept dropped from ~2.1s to ~1.1s — pre-positioned defenders
engage attackers before they reach the spread targets.

## The precise finding: it's the ENV, not the retraining
Both models in this eval ran in the new distributed-spawn env, and BOTH score
93–95% on direct raids — including aegis_splitdirect, which was trained with
clustered spawns (it scored only 83–85% in the old clustered-spawn env last
tick). So **the pre-positioning is an environment property that helps any split
policy**, not a learned behavior that needs retraining. The structural diagnosis
was exactly right.

## Decision
Adopt **`aegis_splitspawn`** as the split champion — trained consistent with the
now-default distributed-spawn geometry, K=3 direct 90–95%, saturation 100%,
single-target 100%. The split-vs-direct weakness, the last uneven cell in the
n=100 scorecard, is closed (74–81% → 93–95%). Distributed spawns are now the
default for multi-target (K=1 unchanged/bit-identical).

## Artifacts
Champion `aegis_splitspawn`; replay `aegis_splitspawn_15v15.gif`. 256 worlds
(Gemma probe coexisting), no OOM.

# Split-vs-Direct Gap — Targeted Fine-Tune (2026-08-30, DD-17)

**Gap:** K=3 split defense handled saturation raids at 100% but DIRECT
simultaneous raids at only 74-81% (the one uneven cell in the n=100 scorecard).
Fine-tuned aegis_split with direct raids weighted 3× → `aegis_splitdirect`.

## Result: gap HALVED, no regression → adopt as split champion
| K=3 cell | direct: old → new | saturation |
|---|---|---|
| 10v10 | 81% → 83% (+2) | 100% (unchanged) |
| 15v15 | 78% → **85%** (+7) | 100% |
| 20v20 | 74% → **83%** (+9) | 100% |
| Single-target K=1 (regression) | **100%** (perfect, no loss) | 100% |

Biggest gains on the hardest cells (+9pt at 20v20), leakers down (0.30→0.20),
zero regression on saturation raids or single-target. `aegis_splitdirect` is a
strict improvement over `aegis_split` — **adopted as the split champion.**

## Honest finding: the residual gap is STRUCTURAL, not training-exposure
Direct-heavy training closed ~half the gap but plateaued at 83-85%, not 100%.
The remaining ~15pt is a coordination limit: defenders spawn clustered on a ring
around the target CENTROID, so against an all-at-once rush on 3 spread points they
can't pre-position fast enough — some attackers reach outer targets before a
defender arrives. Training exposure teaches better commitment but can't overcome
the spawn geometry. **The lever to close the rest is structural: distribute
defender spawns across the K targets** (a spawn change), not more training.
Logged as the next multi-target improvement.

## Verdict
The one measured weakness is materially reduced (direct-raid win +6pt average, to
83-85%) with no cost elsewhere. Split defense is now uniformly strong except the
hardest direct-rush case, whose residual is understood and has a clear next fix.

# Multi-Target Split Defense (2026-08-29, DD-17) — NEW CAPABILITY, works

Can the swarm protect **3 separate points at once**? Implemented with zero
allocator/interception change (num_targets=1 stays bit-identical): attackers
split across K spread targets, each breaches its own, and because defenders are
assigned to *attackers* (now heading to different points) they fan out to cover
the whole field. Fine-tuned `aegis_split` from big24 at K=3.

## It works — and beats the zero-shot baseline
| Model @ K=3 (3 points, 10v10–20v20) | Win | Leakers | Bad-death |
|---|---|---|---|
| **aegis_split (trained on K=3)** | **0.90** | **0.11** | **4.5%** |
| big24 (zero-shot at K=3) | 0.87 | 0.15 | 8.4% |

Two findings: (1) **big24 already generalizes to split defense zero-shot** (0.87)
— the ego-centric obs + allocator transfer to multiple targets with no training,
another payoff of the size/structure-invariant design. (2) Fine-tuning still
helps meaningfully: +3 pt win, lower leakers, and **half the attrition** (better
coverage discipline learned from the harder task). Gate: 0.89 win / 10.0
intercepts per episode (vs ~7 single-target — more spread threats to catch).

## No regression: strict superset
`aegis_split` at K=1 (single target): **win 1.00, zero leakers** — it lost
nothing on the original task while gaining split defense. It is a superset of
big24, not a specialization.

## Verdict
Split defense is a real, working capability. `aegis_split` handles both 1 and 3
protected points. The architecture extends to K targets essentially for free —
the emergent-coordination design (defenders follow assigned attackers) is what
makes multi-point defense fall out without new machinery.

## Artifacts
Champion `aegis_split` (1-and-3-target); replay `aegis_split3_15v15.gif` (three
gold stars, swarm covering all). 256 worlds (coexisted with the Gemma probe), no OOM.

# Safety-Shaping A/B (2026-08-29, DD-17) — VERDICT: keep big24, do NOT adopt

**Question:** does stronger safety shaping (r_safety_coeff −0.10→−0.15,
obstacle_danger_radius 90→110 m) cut the 16% terrain attrition at high density?
Retrained from big24 at 448 worlds; measured real terrain collisions (bad_death;
the danger radius only scales the shaping reward, not the kill geometry, so the
comparison is fair).

## Result: it's a DENSITY-DEPENDENT TRADEOFF, not a win
| Range | big24 bad-death | safe bad-death | Δ | win |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dense obstacle 10v10–20v20 (trained density) | 9.1% | **5.9%** | **−3.2 pt** | 1.00 both |
| Scale 40v40–50v40 (zero-shot up) | 14.6% | **25.4%** | **+10.9 pt** | 1.00 both |

Stronger avoidance helps in the density it was trained at, but **generalizes
worse to crowded arenas**: at 50v40 the larger 110 m danger rings make 40+ drones
over-avoid and pile up, roughly DOUBLING collisions. Win stays 100% throughout
(reusable interceptors absorb the losses), so this is purely an efficiency
tradeoff.

## Decision
**Keep `aegis_big24` as champion.** Do not adopt the stronger constants as
default — they'd trade the project's headline zero-shot up-scaling (24v24→50v40)
for lower attrition only at the trained density. The stronger-safety model
(`aegis_safe`) is kept for the specific case of operating AT ~10–20v20 density in
heavy terrain, where it's a clean 3-pt improvement.

## The real lesson
Safety shaping tuned at one swarm density does not transfer across densities.
Attrition is better treated as density-matched (train the safety knob for the
deployment density) than globally maximized. Genuine terrain-attrition reduction
at all scales likely needs a density-invariant mechanism (e.g. obstacle-relative
speed limiting, or curriculum over danger_radius), not a fixed stronger coefficient.

# OOM Fix — Validated at Full 512 Worlds (2026-08-29, DD-17)

## The fix (commit 29a874d)
The recurring `exit 137` (4 kills across nights) was NOT RAM exhaustion — steady
RSS was always low. Root cause: `step_wait` allocated a fresh `wp.array()` for
drone velocities EVERY step, and `_upload_resets` reallocated init/mask/mass
arrays on EVERY world reset. At high world counts that churn spiked the unified
(VRAM+RAM) pool into transient OOMs. Fix: preallocate those GPU buffers once,
copy in-place via `.assign()`. Bit-identical (21/21 tests).

## Validation: the config that killed us 4× now runs clean
512 worlds × n_max=20 (10,240 agent streams), 30M-step stage-3 fine-tune:
- **exit 0, NO OOM**, RAM flat ~12 GB throughout (prior runs died ~20M steps in)
- 171k agent-steps/s in a microbench; ~1.1 GB peak host RSS
- Gate: **win 0.97** (256 eps), 7.7 intercepts/episode

## Bonus champion `aegis_oomval` (full-worlds terrain generalist)
| Eval | Win | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Maze 10v10 + 20v20 | **1.00** | matches largeP; terrain deaths 20% (unshaped this run) |
| Scale 40v30 + 50v40 | **1.00** | 90-drone battles, 6% losses |

## Impact: the ≤256-world cap is LIFTED
Every large run since night 1 was throttled to 256/384 worlds to dodge the OOM.
Full 512 worlds (≈2× parallelism, ~2× faster large-N training) is available
again for all future work. This unblocks the bigger ambitions (30v30+, deeper
population loops, larger adversarial pools) at full throughput.

**Question:** the moving-target escort came free vs *scripted* attackers — can a
*learned* attacker exploit the wandering convoy (as one did to the small-team
cf_lab policy)?

## Verdict: escort is adversarially ROBUST out of the box (NO GAP)
40M-step RL attackers vs the frozen `aegis_convoy` escort on 10 m/s moving
targets:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| ROUND A gate (attacker's home turf, 256 eps) | true_breach **0.077** — below the 0.08 gap threshold |
| Verdict | **no gap → round B skipped by design** |
| Grid eval (frozen escort vs the learned attacker, 5v5/8v8/10v12) | win **0.89**, breach **0.016** |

The escort's large-team redundancy (N∈[4,20], unlike cf_lab's 1–4) leaves no
consistent seam for an adaptive attacker — the same reason the large
terrain-generalist was robust first-pass while the small lab policy was not.
The attacker peaked at ~0.08 true breach mid-training but could not push past it;
its residual "success" is mostly stall-timeouts, not real penetration.

## Contrast (the emerging rule)
| Policy | Team size | Adaptive-attacker gap? |
|---|---|---|
| aegis_cf_lab | 1–4 (small) | YES (0.19) → needed hardening |
| aegis_largeP (terrain) | 4–20 | no |
| **aegis_convoy (escort)** | 4–20 | **no** |

**Rule of thumb: small-team policies need an adversarial hardening pass before
deployment; large-team policies inherit robustness structurally.**

# Physical Speed-Brake A/B (2026-08-29, DD-17) — VERDICT: keep big24, do NOT adopt

**Hypothesis:** a physical obstacle-proximity speed brake (dynamics, not reward)
would be density-INVARIANT and cut terrain attrition at all scales — succeeding
where the reward-shaping A/B failed.

## Result: it fails the SAME way, even harder
| Range | big24 bad-death | brake bad-death | Δbad | Δwin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dense 10v10–20v20 (trained density) | 9.1% | **4.7%** | **−4.4 pt** | −2.3 pt |
| Scale 40v40–50v40 (zero-shot up) | 14.6% | **34.5%** | **+19.9 pt** | −1.2 pt |

The brake helps at low/trained density but **DOUBLES attrition at 50v40** — worse
than the reward-shaping approach's +11 pt. Mechanism: at extreme density the
arena is packed with obstacles, so braked defenders are perpetually slowed,
spend LONGER near hazards, and collide MORE. Caution backfires when crowded.

## The confirmed pattern (two independent mechanisms, same failure)
| Attrition mechanism | 20v20 | 50v40 |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger safety shaping (reward) | −3 pt | **+11 pt** |
| Physical speed brake (dynamics) | −4 pt | **+20 pt** |

**Terrain attrition at high swarm density is NOT a caution-tuning problem.**
Making defenders more careful near obstacles — by reward OR by physics — reduces
collisions only when there's room, and backfires in crowded arenas. Since
win stays ~100% at every density (reusable interceptors absorb the losses),
the density-scaling attrition is an intrinsic cost, not a bug to shape away.

## Decision
**Keep `aegis_big24` as champion.** The brake (`obstacle_speed_limit`, default
off, code retained) is useful ONLY for low-density terrain ops (~≤20v20), like
the safety model. If high-density attrition ever truly matters, the lever is
STRUCTURAL (fewer/smaller obstacles, explicit inter-drone spacing, or a
formation-aware allocator), not caution.

## Ops note
448-world AEGIS OOMs (exit 137) when the concurrent non-AEGIS process
`dev/probe_gemma_robust.py` shares the box — retried clean at 256 worlds. Use
≤256 worlds when sharing the GPU.

# Big-Swarm Scale-Up (2026-08-29, DD-17) — enabled by the OOM fix

First run to exploit the lifted ≤256-world cap: a **24v24 generalist trained at
448 worlds** (both beyond the prior 20v20 / 256-384 ceiling). Champion:
`models/aegis_big24_stage3_realism_best/best_model.zip`. Gate win 0.98, RAM flat
~12 GB throughout (the .assign OOM fix, commit 29a874d, is what made it possible).

## Trained range (15v15–24v24, mixed terrain, coordinated raids)
**100% win, zero leakers on every cell/strategy.** Terrain deaths 5–12%
(saturation/decoy in obstacle worlds are the hardest).

## Generalizes UP with ZERO extra training (the headline)
| Cell | Win | Leakers | Bad-death |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30v30 | **100%** | 0.00 | 13% |
| 40v40 | **100%** | 0.00 | 14% |
| 50v40 (90 drones) | **100%** | 0.00 | 16% |
| **20v40 (outnumbered 2:1)** | **100%** | 0.00 | 11% |

A policy trained only to 24v24 defends 40v40 and 50v40 perfectly, and — because
reusable interceptors re-engage — **20 defenders beat 40 attackers with zero
leakers**. Size-invariance now proven from 1v1 (cf_lab) through 50v40 across the
whole model family; the ego-centric obs + allocator carry it.

## Significance
The OOM fix wasn't just reliability — it raised the trainable ceiling. Bigger
training swarms (24v24) produce the same perfect generalization the 20v20
champion did, now with more headroom for future work (deeper population loops,
30v30+ native training). Bad-death rises with swarm density in obstacle worlds
(16% at 50v40); a survival-bonus shaping pass is the lever if that matters.

# Phase-3 Adversarial Co-Training — Round 1 Results (2026-08-20)

Driver: `train/train_aegis_adv.py`. Round A: RL attackers (evade mode, 41-dim ego
obs) vs frozen `aegis_patch` defender, 40M steps. Round B: defender retrained 60M
steps vs mixture (mixed/flanking/jink/rl). Evals: 5 cells (3v5,5v3,5v5,8v5,8v8),
realism, 48 episodes each. CSVs: `models/eval_output/nvm_grid_model_{patch_vs_rl,advdef_vs_rl,advdef_scripted}.csv`.

## (a) Learned attackers DEMOLISH the scripted-era champion
| Old (patch) defender vs | Win | Leakers | Defender bad-deaths |
|---|---|---|---|
| scripted attackers (same cells) | 0.78 | 0.33 | 2.2% |
| **RL attackers** | **0.01** | **4.19** | **94.4%** |

The attackers' discovered exploit is **kamikaze-baiting**: lure pursuing defenders
into hills/ground (94% of defenders die to terrain, not trades), then cruise in.
Attack runs take 22s. A defender that was untouchable by scripted opponents is
essentially disarmed by an adaptive one — this is why Phase 3 exists.

## (b) One co-training round closes much — not all — of the gap
| Co-trained defender vs RL attackers | Old | Co-trained |
|---|---|---|
| Win | 0.01 | **0.24** |
| Leakers/episode | 4.19 | **1.78** |
| Bad-death rate | 94.4% | **19.6%** |
| Episode length | 22s (rout) | 55s (contested full-length fights) |

The co-trained defender stopped taking the bait (bad deaths −75 pts), forces
full-length engagements, and halves the leakage. Parity vs a fully adaptive
attacker needs more self-play iterations — expected; that's the nature of the loop.

## (c) Non-regression vs scripted: clean
Full 114-cell realism grid: co-trained **0.75** vs old 0.76 win (within noise),
bad deaths 2.9% vs 2.5%. **The mixture worked — no catastrophic forgetting.**

## Notes
- Attacker stalling: mid-training the attackers also learned to loiter out of reach
  (timeouts ~50%); the stranded-as-leaker metric counts this as attacker success,
  but from a mission standpoint a stalling attacker is defeated. The Round-B
  defender ignores the bait (timeouts 0 in mixture evals).
- Round-B gate on seed 99 read 0.69 vs the 0.70 target (mixture sampling + stall
  accounting); grid evals above are the meaningful numbers.

## Next iterations (standard population loop)
1. Round A2: train fresh attackers vs the co-trained defender (aegis_adv_defender).
2. Round B2: retrain defender vs pool {scripted, A1, A2} — grow the mixture.
3. Try co-training the **reuse (kamikaze=False)** defender: the baiting exploit
   preys on kamikaze economics (a defender death is worth an attacker's while);
   a reusable interceptor is structurally less baitable.
4. Consider attacker-side stall penalty (time reward) so attacker training pressure
   stays on penetration, not clock-milking.

Models: `models/aegis_adv_attacker_best/`, `models/aegis_adv_defender_best/`.
